Now building

The newest builds, end to end.

I'm always learning something new, then building and delivering it. These are the products I'm currently taking end-to-end, from PRD to launch, driven by curiosity and a self-starter's instinct to go build it. For the bigger launches from my career, scroll down to the Track Record section below.

Problem

Logging lifts in a notepad app was painful with fatigue-shaky thumbs. The workout apps were worse: an account to create, then logging buried under features I never asked for. I wanted one tap, not four. My design bar: a 70-year-old lifter should record a set without figuring anything out.

What I did

Wrote a full PRD: goals, in scope versus out, user flows, use cases, success metrics, UX and functional requirements. Framed every flow in Figma, built in Replit, and learned both tools on the way. The result has no login screen and one job: record weights, see progression.

What I cut, and why

Automatic progression prompts and goal-based exercise recommendations. Both are appealing, and both risk injury when software makes the call without knowing a person's body. That judgment belongs to a trainer, or to a far more careful design than a v1 timeline allows. Cutting them is what let me deliver in a week.

Status & what's next

In final QA, moving through Google Play's two-week production review. First measures after launch: time to log a first set, and week-one return rate. Progression suggestions return only when I can design them safely.

The thesis

Tired of deciding what to wear every morning, I wanted to save my decision-making for things that matter. So I printed a shirt that said "My Work Shirt." Strangers laughed, coworkers asked where I got it, and the signal was clear. Katakataku sells shirts for the exact moment we live in. No motivational quotes, no graphics, just plain and relatable.

Zero to launch, solo

One week from idea to a live shop with 30 designs. All of it mine: brand, every design, the print-on-demand supply chain, listings and search copy, the storefront. No team, no budget, just a hypothesis and the willingness to learn each tool it needed.

What I'm measuring from day one

The catalog is my backlog and Etsy search is my acquisition funnel. I track impressions → views → favorites → orders per listing, plus contribution margin per SKU after costs and fees. Every catalog decision follows the same loop: hypothesis, change, before/after data, decision. Early numbers will be small, and I would rather read them honestly than wait for big ones.

Principles

How I work.

Ambiguity is the starting line.

Priorities shift and requirements evolve. That's the job, not an obstacle. I create clarity: define the metric, scope the work, sequence the dependencies, and get teams moving.

Data over opinions.

Decisions trace back to data and business objectives: define the metric up front, read it honestly, and let it settle the debates that opinions can't.

Prototype speed is a skill.

Tools like Figma and Replit mean a PM can validate with a working product, not a slide deck. I learned both mid-project because the project needed them.

Write the decision down.

PRDs, experiment logs, risk lists. If a decision isn't written down with its rationale, it can't be reviewed, defended, or learned from.
